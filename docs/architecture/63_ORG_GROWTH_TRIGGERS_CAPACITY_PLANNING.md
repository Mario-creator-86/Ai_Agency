# Organizational Growth Triggers & Capacity Planning

## Goal

Let the CEO grow the company when needed, while avoiding uncontrolled agent/department proliferation.

## Need score

A department/role creation proposal should consider:

- queue backlog;
- SLA/SLO misses;
- error/rework rate;
- direct-report count;
- workload concentration;
- new channel/platform;
- risk exposure;
- marketing/community volume;
- finance complexity;
- legal review volume;
- provider complexity;
- growth opportunity;
- budget headroom.

## Possible trigger examples

### HR
If active agents > configurable threshold OR hires/fires exceed threshold.

### Finance
If monthly external spend > threshold OR multiple paid channels/providers exist.

### Legal
If sponsor/merch/paid ads activate OR content risk volume rises.

### Market Intelligence
If channel reaches sufficient analytics volume OR CEO wants expansion into new markets.

### Paid Advertising
If organic funnel is proven and paid budget is approved.

These are default heuristics, not hard business rules.

## Trial role first

Before creating a full department, CEO should often:
1. create specialist role;
2. run trial;
3. measure value;
4. then create department if workload persists.

## Capacity planning

Track:
- agent concurrency;
- tasks/day;
- queue age;
- cost/task;
- coordination messages;
- manager span of control;
- department throughput.

## Org bloat defense

Trigger review if:
- too many idle agents;
- department output not measurable;
- coordination overhead exceeds value;
- duplicate role charters;
- spend rises without content/business impact.

CEO may:
- merge;
- dissolve;
- reassign;
- suspend;
- reduce scope.

## Story value is secondary

A new role/department can create good serial content, but narrative entertainment alone is not sufficient operational reason to create costly real agents unless Owner explicitly chooses a story-first mode.


# V5 Financial growth triggers

Department growth/hiring proposals should consider:
- current VR$ runway;
- recurring provider costs;
- trailing settled revenue;
- revenue concentration;
- forecast confidence;
- debt service;
- expected role ROI.

A successful channel does not automatically justify unlimited headcount.
