# Legal, Rights & Compliance Department

## Purpose

Review content, marketing, IP, rights, sponsorship and public communications for elevated risk.

This may start as a Rights/Policy Reviewer and later become a department.

## Possible roles

- Legal/Compliance Lead
- Content Rights Reviewer
- Platform Policy Reviewer
- Trademark/Brand Reviewer
- Advertising Disclosure Reviewer
- Privacy Reviewer

## Boundary

These agents provide automated risk analysis and policy gates.

They are not a substitute for licensed legal counsel on high-stakes or jurisdiction-specific questions.

## Review scopes

- factual claims with legal sensitivity;
- copyright/licensing;
- music/media rights;
- AI-generated asset rights;
- trademark/brand usage;
- use of provider/model names as characters;
- merchandise;
- sponsorship disclosures;
- paid promotion;
- privacy;
- public fan submissions;
- platform rules;
- defamation/reputation risk;
- contests/giveaways later.

## Character/IP warning

Internal employees may use technical identities like model/provider names.

Public-facing character branding should be configurable as:
- technical name;
- alias;
- original fictional persona.

Do not assume the studio can commercially merchandise another company's trademark or character identity.

Legal/Brand review should trigger before:
- merch;
- sponsorship;
- paid campaign;
- trademark-heavy public branding.

## Video gate

Legal/Compliance can output:
- PASS;
- PASS_WITH_NOTICE;
- NEEDS_REWORK;
- ESCALATE_TO_OWNER;
- EXTERNAL_COUNSEL_RECOMMENDED;
- BLOCK.

CEO cannot override a hard rights/policy block if configured as hard gate.

## Marketing coordination

All high-risk campaigns may route:
Marketing
→ Legal/Compliance
→ Publish.

## Serial coordination

Internal legal disputes or reviews can become story material only after public sanitization.

Do not reveal sensitive legal advice, complaints, credentials or private Owner matters.


# V5 Economy Legal Scope

Legal reviews:
- paid pitch/donation terms;
- sponsorship financial disclosures;
- public claims about revenue;
- virtual financing language so it is not represented as a real loan offer;
- branded products/assets selected by VendorShop;
- public display of product marks/logos;
- privacy of financial data.

Legal cannot convert a virtual agreement into a binding real-world contract.
