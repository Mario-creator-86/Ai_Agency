# Marketing Attribution & Experimentation

## Goal

Learn which marketing actions actually increase reach, episode views, community engagement and channel growth.

## Experiment object

Fields:
- hypothesis;
- campaign;
- platform;
- control;
- variants;
- target metric;
- guardrail metrics;
- sample plan;
- start/end;
- attribution method;
- result;
- confidence;
- decision.

## Metrics hierarchy

Primary examples:
- qualified episode views;
- watch time;
- completion;
- subscriber/follower growth;
- returning viewers;
- community engagement.

Secondary:
- impressions;
- likes;
- CTR;
- comments;
- shares;
- link clicks.

Guardrails:
- negative sentiment;
- unsubscribe/unfollow;
- spam reports;
- policy strikes;
- misleading-click complaints.

## Cross-platform causality

Do not overclaim.

Possible evidence levels:
1. direct platform conversion;
2. tagged link;
3. matched time-series lift;
4. correlation only;
5. unknown.

## CEO/CMO learning

Strategy changes need:
- sufficient sample;
- effect size;
- cost;
- repeatability;
- platform specificity.

Example:
"Conflict teasers outperformed mystery teasers on TikTok" should not automatically become Instagram policy without evidence.

## Clickbait quality score

Possible dimensions:
- curiosity;
- truth;
- payoff;
- specificity;
- brand fit;
- audience fatigue.

A hook with high clicks but poor payoff/retention should be penalized.


# V5 Revenue attribution

Where possible, link settled revenue to:
- platform;
- channel;
- content item;
- campaign;
- paid pitch/donation source;
- sponsor.

Do not overclaim causal attribution when platform data only supports correlation.

Marketing metrics may influence revenue forecasts, but only Finance/RevenueEvidence settlement can create VR$.
