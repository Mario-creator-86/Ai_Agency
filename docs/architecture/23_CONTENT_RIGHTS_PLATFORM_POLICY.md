# Content Rights, Safety and Platform Policy

## Goal

Keep the studio sellable, monetizable and defensible.

## Asset provenance

Every external/generated asset should record:
- origin;
- provider;
- URL/source if applicable;
- license/usage basis;
- generation terms reference if relevant;
- content item;
- timestamp.

## Music

Do not use random copyrighted music automatically.

Use:
- explicitly licensed library;
- platform-approved source;
- generated music with verified usage rights;
- or no music.

## Voice

Track TTS provider/voice usage terms and voice identity.

Avoid unauthorized impersonation.

## Stock / archival footage

Store rights/license basis.

If no usable rights:
do not publish.

## AI visualization labels

Where necessary distinguish:
- archival/real footage;
- simulation;
- illustration;
- AI-generated visualization.

Do not present synthetic depictions as authentic documentary footage.

## Current platform rules

Platform policies change.

Before enabling full autonomous publishing, the implementation must verify current official YouTube/Google policy relevant to:
- synthetic/altered content;
- repetitive/reused content;
- spam;
- misleading metadata;
- monetization eligibility;
- upload quotas/automation.

Do not hardcode old policy assumptions into permanent logic without a review mechanism.

## High-risk factual categories

Initial content strategy should prefer low-risk educational topics.

If later entering:
- medical;
- financial;
- legal;
- politics/current conflicts;
- allegations about real persons;
apply stricter review/approval policy.

## Fan submissions future

Paid submission does not guarantee publication.

Must include:
- moderation;
- content policy;
- payment terms;
- spam/abuse protection.

## Third-party model/provider branding

Using provider/model names as public characters, channel branding or merchandise can create trademark/endorsement concerns.

Support:
- internal technical name;
- public fictional alias;
- attribution policy;
- brand-use review state.

Do not assume a provider's name/logo can be printed on merchandise.

## Dependency/content license inventory

Track licenses for:
- fonts;
- sprite packs;
- stock assets;
- music libraries;
- code dependencies where distribution obligations matter.

## Source reuse

Researching a source does not automatically grant the right to reuse its images/video/text.

Evidence citation and media licensing are separate questions.

## Terms snapshots

For commercial-critical providers, record:
- terms/policy reference;
- last reviewed date;
- notes affecting generated-content commercial use.

Do not attempt legal interpretation automatically beyond configured rules; escalate uncertain commercial rights.

## Takedown/correction process

Maintain operational path for:
- rights complaint;
- factual correction;
- platform warning;
- removed asset.

Be able to map published video → source asset/provider/provenance quickly.


## V4 marketing/legal additions

Before cross-platform campaign publication verify:
- platform-specific policy;
- ad/sponsor disclosure;
- trademark usage;
- source asset rights;
- music rights;
- synthetic-media disclosure if required;
- public event sanitization.

Using real AI provider/model names as public characters may create trademark/brand/merch risk.

Support public aliases/original characters and route commercialization questions to Legal/Brand review.
